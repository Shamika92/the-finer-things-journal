<blockquote class="poem">
<p>Brew me a cup for a winter’s night.
For the wind howls loud, and the furies fight;
Spice it with love and stir it with care,
And I’ll toast your bright eyes, my sweetheart fair.</p>
<cite>Minna Thomas Antrim, “A Night Cap,” <em>A Book of Toasts</em>, 1902</cite>
</blockquote>

Even when winter starts at different times in different countries and continents, people have always enjoyed savouring it with lights, warmth, and the comfort of food and drink.

Here is a small collection of interesting drinks from around the world that will make you want to go and grab one for your day.

## 1. Salep

<p class="meta">Turkey</p>

<figure>
  <div class="grain"><img src="/images/journal/6-cups-of-winter-around-the-world/salep.jpg" alt="A white china cup of salep dusted with cinnamon, on a wooden board with a small wooden spoon" loading="lazy" /></div>
</figure>

A thick, creamy milk drink traditionally made with flour from orchid roots and dusted with cinnamon. It takes thousands of wild orchid tubers to make a single kilo of that flour, which is why Turkey has banned the export of the tubers and why many sachets today use starch instead.

*Taste: Thick, creamy, and warmly spiced*

## 2. Amazake

<p class="meta">Japan</p>

<figure>
  <div class="grain"><img src="/images/journal/6-cups-of-winter-around-the-world/amazake.jpg" alt="A dark ceramic cup of cloudy white amazake beside a woven basket of rice" loading="lazy" /></div>
</figure>

Amazake is a traditional Japanese fermented rice drink with a thick, porridge-like consistency. In the Edo period it was a summer drink, sold cold by street vendors to beat the heat, and its name even became a summer word in haiku. Today it is poured warm at shrines on New Year’s Eve.

*Taste: Smooth, sweet, and mildly nutty*

## 3. Ponche Navideño

<p class="meta">Mexico</p>

<figure class="narrow">
  <div class="grain"><img src="/images/journal/6-cups-of-winter-around-the-world/ponche-navideno.jpg" alt="A clay pot of ponche with a stick of sugarcane, beside tejocotes, a cone of piloncillo, sugarcane and cinnamon sticks" loading="lazy" /></div>
</figure>

It is a traditional, fragrant Mexican hot fruit punch typically simmered in large pots and ladled into mugs along with chunks of softened fruit and a stick of fresh sugarcane. The small golden fruit in it are tejocotes, a Mexican hawthorn, and the punch is served through Las Posadas, the nine nights before Christmas.

*Taste: Tart, spiced, and deeply fruity*

## 4. Noon Chai

<p class="meta">Kashmir, India</p>

<figure class="narrow">
  <div class="grain"><img src="/images/journal/6-cups-of-winter-around-the-world/noon-chai.jpg" alt="A glass cup of pink noon chai topped with cinnamon and crushed chocolate on a gold-rimmed saucer" loading="lazy" /></div>
</figure>

A beautiful pink tea made with tea leaves, milk, salt, and a little baking soda. A comforting everyday tradition in Kashmir’s cold climate. The baking soda turns the brew deep red as it boils, and the milk softens it to pink. It is traditionally made in a copper samovar and served with girda, a Kashmiri bread.

*Taste: Rich, creamy, and mildly salty*

## 5. Sujeonggwa

<p class="meta">South Korea</p>

<figure>
  <div class="grain"><img src="/images/journal/6-cups-of-winter-around-the-world/sujeonggwa.jpg" alt="A dark bowl of sujeonggwa with pine nuts and dried persimmon, beside persimmon halves, cinnamon sticks, star anise and ginger on a wooden tray" loading="lazy" /></div>
</figure>

A fragrant cinnamon-and-ginger punch with dried persimmons and pine nuts, traditionally served during celebrations including Korean New Year. Despite its warming spices, it is usually served chilled, as a dessert drink. The dried persimmons are the same <a href="/journal/the-finer-edit-november-2026">gotgam from our November edition</a>.

*Taste: Sweet, aromatic, and sharply spiced*

## 6. Bicerin

<p class="meta">Turin, Italy</p>

<figure>
  <div class="grain"><img src="/images/journal/6-cups-of-winter-around-the-world/bicerin.jpg" alt="A tall glass of bicerin in layers of espresso, chocolate and cream, beside a chocolate croissant in a Turin café" loading="lazy" /></div>
</figure>

A luxurious little glass of espresso, hot chocolate, and cream. Traditionally served in distinct layers, it’s meant to be enjoyed without stirring. The name is Piedmontese for “little glass”, and the Turin café said to have invented it has been serving it since 1763. Its exact recipe is a closely guarded secret.

*Taste: Decadent, creamy, and bittersweet*
